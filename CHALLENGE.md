# The Fort Mason Address Challenge 🏁

**An open AI gauntlet with a real-world finish line:**
*Fix the addresses in major map systems for Fort Mason.*

For almost a decade, deliveries to the homes inside Fort Mason, San Francisco, have gone to the wrong house. Packages
for one home routinely land at another house 250 m away; drivers give up and photograph a box on somebody else's porch. Google mostly
copes. Apple Maps, many delivery apps and the government's own geocoder don't.

We know *why*, roughly (see the [README](README.md)): Fort Mason has two address systems for the same buildings, and one
of them collides with real addresses 3.7 km away. Knowing why hasn't fixed it. **This challenge is about getting it
fixed**, in the real systems, measured by a machine.

## The finish line: the scoreboard

[`SCOREBOARD.md`](SCOREBOARD.md) is regenerated by [`scripts/scoreboard.py`](scripts/scoreboard.py). For each Fort
Mason address ([`data/truth/fort-mason.csv`](data/truth/fort-mason.csv)) it asks each map system *"where is this?"*.
The answer passes if it lands within **60 m of the right building**.

Baseline, 2026-10-05:

| System | Score |
|---|---|
| Apple Maps | not tested yet (needs an Apple Maps Server API key) |
| Google | not tested yet (needs a key) |
| **US Census geocoder** | **0 / 24** |
| OpenStreetMap Nominatim | 18 / 24 (2, 3 and 4 Fort Mason land 290–370 m off) |
| Photon (OSM) | 23 / 24 |

**A system is "fixed" when its column is fully green and stays green for 30 days.** Next in line: Apple Maps forward
*and* reverse ("My Location"), HERE, TomTom, Esri and Mapbox, and the delivery apps through field reports.

## How to play

Anyone can enter: people, AI agents, or (most likely) people with agents. Typical moves:

- **Diagnose:** prove *which* upstream source feeds each wrong answer, for example tracing Apple's result to Census
  TIGER, CoreLogic parcels or OSM, with evidence.
- **Fix at the source,** through the legitimate channel:
  - an OpenStreetMap tagging fix such as `addr:place`;
  - a well-documented request to San Francisco's addressing team (EAS);
  - a correction through Apple's, HERE's or TomTom's own processes.
- **Extend the scoreboard:** add a system, add reverse geocoding, add delivery-app field tests.
- **Widen the map:** run [`scripts/stranded.py`](scripts/stranded.py) on another Bay Area place with the same disease.
  Early candidates: the Presidio (its own Montgomery and Mason Streets collide with downtown addresses), Treasure Island,
  Hunters Point Shipyard, Fort Baker, the Marin Headlands.

Open a pull request or an issue. Every claim needs evidence that someone else can re-run.

## Rules (the spirit: be the kind of contributor these systems want more of)

1. **Legitimate channels only.** No spam, no fake accounts, no mass automated submissions to report forms, and no
   impersonating residents, the City, the National Park Service or companies. One accurate, well-evidenced report beats
   a thousand noisy ones, and noisy ones hurt everybody here.
2. **OpenStreetMap norms apply.** Follow OSM tagging conventions and the local community. Discuss bulk edits before
   making them, and never import commercial map data.
3. **Respect the services' terms.** Don't bulk-scrape or republish commercial geocoder results. The scoreboard stores
   only PASS/FAIL and distance.
4. **Residents' privacy comes first.** No names, phone numbers or photos of people. Anecdotes are published only with
   consent and without personal details.
5. **Disclose AI use** in your PR or report. Agents are welcome; a human stays accountable for every submission to an
   outside organisation.
6. **Show your work.** Wins count only when the scoreboard shows them and the path is documented, so the next
   neighbourhood can repeat it.
