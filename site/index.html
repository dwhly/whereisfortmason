The problem

Order something to a home inside Fort Mason and there's a good chance it lands at the wrong door. Packages for one house are routinely left at another house 250 m away. Drivers give up, leave the box on a stranger's porch and photograph it. Google mostly copes; Apple Maps, many delivery apps and the US government's own geocoder don't.

Why: Fort Mason has two address systems for the same buildings.

  • Residents and the post office use "3 Fort Mason".
  • The City's official address database calls the same spot "3 Franklin St, 94123".
  • "3 Franklin St" also exists 3.7 km away at Market Street.

Lose the ZIP code and you're downtown. Search for "3 Fort Mason" and the government geocoder finds nothing at all.

How to play

People, AI agents, or people with agents. Every claim needs evidence someone else can re-run.

  1. Diagnose: prove which upstream data source feeds each wrong answer.
  2. Fix at the source, through the legitimate channel: an OpenStreetMap tagging fix, a well-documented request to the City's addressing team, or Apple's, HERE's or TomTom's own correction processes.
  3. Extend the scoreboard: add Apple Maps search and "My Location", Google, HERE, TomTom, Esri and Mapbox, and delivery-app field tests.
  4. Widen the map: find the next stranded neighbourhood.

Rules

  • Legitimate channels only: no spam, no fake accounts, no mass automated report submissions, no impersonation.
  • OpenStreetMap norms apply; discuss bulk edits first, and never import commercial data.
  • Respect the services' terms: no bulk scraping or republishing of commercial results.
  • Residents' privacy comes first.
  • Disclose AI use. A human is accountable for every outside submission.
  • Wins count when the scoreboard shows them and the path is documented.
Prizes: milestone prizes, announced soon. First system flipped to green · the Apple Maps flip · best root-cause proof · each new neighbourhood fixed.

It's not just Fort Mason

Former military and federal sites around San Francisco show the same disease. Our detector compares the addresses people use (OpenStreetMap) with the City's official points. These are candidates, to be confirmed on the ground. In the Presidio, for example, "100 Montgomery St" exists both there and in the Financial District, 5.2 km apart.

Live in Fort Mason?

Your stories are the evidence. Send the organiser, on the Fort Mason neighbours chat:

  • your address exactly as it appears on your mail;
  • what apps or drivers think it is;
  • what goes wrong: which company, how often, where packages end up;
  • screenshots, if you have them.

Nothing is published without your OK, and never with your name.
